# Everything around the main program is a commercial break

Program means the main program only: the dramas, news, variety and other content listed in the TV guide, from its opening to the end of its own ending (theme song and ending credits). Everything the channel airs around it belongs to a commercial break: commercials, promos, sponsor credits, station IDs, next-episode previews and coming-up-next cards. A sponsor card counts as commercial break whenever it is on screen, even when it is laid over the program. Two exceptions count as program even though they advertise: in-program advertising (「PR」 segments, product placement), because it happens inside the main program, and whole-slot shopping programs (通販番組), because they are programs in their own right.

The rule comes from watching 20 examples from the pilot recordings. Sponsor credits, station IDs, previews and coming-up-next cards all felt like part of the commercial break, wherever they sat. It also keeps each commercial break one unbroken run, from where one main program stops to where the next one starts.

## Considered options

An earlier version of this rule counted sponsor credits, station IDs and next-episode previews as program, so that no commercial break would be only a few seconds long. It didn't match what a viewer sees. It also cut stretches of "program" a few seconds long into the middle of commercial breaks, which no live detector could catch in time either.

## Consequences

- Every label depends on this rule, so changing it later means relabeling every recording.
- A station ID or sponsor credit alone between two parts of a program is a commercial break only a few seconds long, too short for a live detector to catch in time. None were found in the pilot recordings.
